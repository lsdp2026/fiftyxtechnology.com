CAPABILITY
What we do

Built for the parts other shops decline.

Our machining discipline was developed on components that operate thousands of feet underground, where a sealing surface, thread, or bore that is almost right is a failure. That standard now serves buyers in aerospace, defense, energy, and industrial markets who need a manufacturer that can take a difficult print and own it end to end.

01   Turning

Multi-axis turning and mill-turn

Turning centers with live tooling and sub-spindle capability complete the front and back of a part in one handling. Fewer setups mean tighter concentricity, better repeatability, and less opportunity for error on complex turned components.

  • Long, slender, and thin-walled turned parts
  • Cross-holes, flats, and milled features in the same setup
  • Precision threads, bores, and sealing surfaces
02   Milling

Precision milling

Multi-axis milling for prismatic components, housings, fixtures, and detail work where flatness, position, and surface finish are called out and inspected. Programmed, fixtured, and proven in-house before production begins.

  • Tight-tolerance pockets, bores, and datum structures
  • Complex contours and compound angles
  • Fixture design and workholding built here
03   Automation

Robotic machine tending

Collaborative robots load, unload, and stage parts so spindles keep cutting through unattended hours. For repeat production, that translates into consistent cycle-to-cycle quality, predictable capacity, and delivery dates we can commit to.

  • Lights-out and extended-shift production
  • Stable pricing on established part numbers
  • Capacity that scales with your release schedule
04   Materials

Difficult materials, done routinely

Work-hardening stainless, gummy copper alloys, and tough nickel alloys behave differently at the cutting edge. We select tooling, speeds, coolant, and workholding for the material, not for the catalog, and we hold that recipe once it is proven.

  • Non-magnetic and austenitic stainless steels
  • Beryllium copper and other high-strength copper alloys
  • Nickel-based alloys, alloy and tool steels, aluminum
05   Engineering

Process engineering

Every job starts with a print review and a manufacturing plan: tooling, workholding, sequence, inspection points, and secondary operations. Programming and prove-out happen on our floor, so first articles arrive with a process behind them, not a guess.

  • Design-for-manufacturability feedback before you commit
  • Documented first-article inspection
  • Locked process for repeat releases
06   Finishing

Finished, not just machined

Heat treat, plating, coatings, marking, and assembly are managed through vendors we have qualified and hold accountable. You issue one purchase order and receive one finished, inspected, certified part, with the paperwork to match.

  • Secondary operations coordinated and inspected on return
  • Certifications and traceability assembled in one package

How we work

A second source that behaves like a first.

Whether you are qualifying a new supplier, moving work off an overloaded shop, or looking for a domestic manufacturer you can actually reach on the phone, the engagement is the same: a clear quote, a proven process, and a delivery date we treat as a promise.

Prototype and first article

One to a few pieces, built with production intent. We review the print for manufacturability, build the process once, and deliver a first article with documentation so the transition to production is a release, not a restart.

Repeat production

Established part numbers with locked programs, fixtures, and inspection plans. Blanket orders, scheduled releases, and stocking agreements are welcome. Automation keeps pricing and lead times stable as volume grows.

Made to print

Your drawing, your specification, your revision control. We manufacture to the print, hold the certifications, and treat your design as confidential. Customer-supplied material and outside-processed parts are handled the same way.

What to send for a fast, accurate quote

The more of this we receive up front, the sooner the quote lands and the fewer questions come back.

  1. Drawing as PDF, plus a STEP or native model when available
  2. Material and specification, or a note that we may recommend one
  3. Quantity now, and the expected annual usage
  4. Required date, and whether a partial release helps
  5. Finish, plating, heat treat, and marking requirements
  6. Certification and inspection documentation required
