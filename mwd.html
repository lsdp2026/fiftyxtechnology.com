Why it matters

More than a machine shop that happens to make MWD parts.

An MWD tool contains dozens of mechanical components that have to work together downhole. Some are large and obvious. Others are small internal pieces that shut down the whole tool when they are worn, damaged, or on backorder.

Most of them are not one-off inventions. The industry has settled on established part families around common tool configurations, and those families get replaced constantly as tools are built, repaired, and returned to service. That makes availability as important as capability. We are structured for both.

Repeat parts run like repeat parts.

Programs, tooling, fixturing, inspection plans, and setup sheets stay on file for every component we have made. When your reorder comes in, it goes to the machine, not back to engineering. That is what lets common families sit on the shelf, and what keeps your variation in material, connection, port, or length from turning into a project.

Find the family. Confirm the variation. Keep the tool moving.

This page is not a claim that every part fits every tool. It exists so you can identify the component family you need and start a technical conversation without explaining the whole assembly from scratch. We work from a part number, a customer print, a prior order, a physical sample, a photo, a marked-up drawing, or a description of where the part lives. When the exact configuration is unclear, we pin down the critical details before anything reaches the machine.

SLEEVE REPAIR

The welds customers ask about.

Three repair zones on a muleshoe sleeve: the top threads, the bore, and the bottom face. Every one is fully fused, cleaned to parent metal, and re-machined to the applicable dimensions. Nothing is ground to hide anything. Customers who have run tools for twenty years tell us they have not seen sleeves come back like this, and we intend to keep it that way.

Why the sleeve matters

The muleshoe sleeve supports and seats the MWD probe, establishes its orientation within the UBHO, and carries critical flow and retention components. Wear, washout, damaged seating surfaces, distorted features, and inconsistent prior repairs all affect alignment, sealing, and tool reliability.

Repair and rebuild

Every repair starts with an incoming evaluation of the complete assembly. Depending on condition, the sleeve is disassembled, straightened, welded, re-machined, fitted with replacement components, and restored to the applicable dimensional requirements. Critical profiles, sealing surfaces, alignment features, retention areas, and repaired sections are inspected before release.

  • Incoming evaluation and documented inspection
  • Precision welding and dimensional restoration
  • Repair of worn, washed, or damaged features
  • Replacement of wear cuffs, keys, retainers, and related parts
  • Re-machining of sealing, seating, and alignment surfaces
  • Final dimensional verification and permanent identification
